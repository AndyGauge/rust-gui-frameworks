Six of the pages before this one cover projects that did not exist on crates.io, or anywhere, eighteen months ago. Taken together, they show where the Rust GUI field moved in 2026.

**Agents are now part of the audience.** Rune runs every app headlessly so that "an agent can render to PNG, dump the element tree and drive it with scripted input", and it takes issues from agents in place of pull requests. Blinc ships a `Skills.md` for coding agents, NSH publishes an `llms.txt` and pitches run-time TSX for "UI generated by AI", and zgui exposes headless probes and counters. Testability without a window was once a niche feature. In 2026 frameworks advertise it as a headline.

**shadcn/ui is the default widget set.** zgui-ui, Blinc's `cn`, gpui-component and NSH's Tailwind classes all copy the same web design system. New toolkits no longer launch with grey buttons. They launch with cards, sheets, toasts and design tokens. The look of a Rust desktop app in 2026 is the look of a 2024 web app.

**CSS is back, without the browser.** Blitz and zgui both run Stylo, Servo's real style engine, and FerroUI brings Avalonia's selectors and control themes. Each is a renderer that understands CSS but contains no web engine, an alternative to embedding a webview the way Tauri does.

**The bottom of the stack held, and the middle kept spreading.** Every new framework that uses winit landed on **0.30.13**. That makes three more frameworks on the same window library, while winit 0.31 remains in beta. wgpu spread further: Blinc pins 26.0.1 exactly, zgui and NSH use 29, and Rune uses 30.0.1 with a patched HAL. Taffy 0.15 (Rune and NSH) is newer than anything in the September table. The newcomers also disagree about everything above the window: zgui writes its own layout engine, Blinc its own text stack, Rune its own renderer, and FerroUI and NSH use none of the shared layers.

**Companies arrived.** Through 2025 most of the frameworks in this book came from individuals, a few companies (System76, Zed, SixtyFPS/Slint) and the Linebender collective. The 2026 newcomers include Anomaly (Rune), Longbridge (NSH), Microsoft (windows-reactor) and Qt (qtbridge), and Dioxus's team joined Cognition. Funding is no longer the scarce resource. Accessibility still is: Blinc has none, and FerroUI and NSH defer to the host platform.

Smaller projects from the same period are also worth watching. **Tessera** (crates.io since July 2025, `tessera-ui` 2.5.0) is a declarative, functional toolkit, although its last crates.io release was in January 2026. **Ply** (`ply-engine`, March 2026) bills itself as an "app engine". **QuickGUI** (August 2026) uses one GPU renderer for Go, TypeScript and Rust, and calls itself "very experimental". Most new frameworks never reach a second year. The history of Druid suggests that the parts worth keeping are the shared layers, more than the frameworks built on them.
